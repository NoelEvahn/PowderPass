# Backrooms audio pack: manifest

All audio in this pack (`pack/java`, `pack/bedrock`, `src/main/resources/backrooms-pack.zip`,
`src/main/resources/bedrock-skeleton.zip (the plugin adds the Ogg files from the Java pack)`) was **generated by this project** from the scripts in
`tools/audio/`, with one exception: `sounds/user/bed_level0.ogg` (`bed.level0`) is a real recording of
fluorescent lights supplied by the server owner (from the "Backrooms PBR Resource Pack" by Pro_fessional,
packsmc.com). Its license is unknown and the owner must confirm redistribution permission. It is listed with that
note in the table, and the original `bed.level0_synth` (CC0) can replace it. Apart from that file, nothing in the
pack is a third-party recording, sample, loop or soundfont. No copyrighted music is included or imitated. The asset table below is regenerated by `tools/audio/build_pack.py`.

**License: every built-in asset is released under CC0 1.0 Universal (public domain dedication)** by the
PowderPass project. Tracks added later by a server owner (see "Adding your own tracks") keep their own license,
which is listed in their table row.

## How the sounds are made

* **Synthesis** (`tools/audio/dsp.py`, `sfx.py`): oscillators and additive harmonic stacks, white/pink/brown
  noise, Butterworth/RBJ filters, modal (decaying-sinusoid) resonator banks for wood, metal, glass and ceramic,
  stick-slip impulse trains for creaks and drags, Minnaert-bubble chirps for water, AM/FM, saturation, and a
  variable delay line for tape wow and flutter.
* **Acoustic profiles**: offline convolution with synthetic impulse responses. Each IR is built from
  band-split exponentially decaying noise with its own RT60 per band, plus explicit early reflections:
  `carpet` (RT60 about 0.3 s, heavy HF damping), `office` (about 0.6 s), `concrete` (about 1.8 s, nine bright
  early reflections), `pool` (about 3.5 s, dense, darker highs), `corridor` (a 21 ms flutter-echo train,
  about 0.9 s), `open` (nearly dry, faint slap at about 170 ms). `muffled` means a 4th-order 600 Hz low-pass,
  -6 dB and a little dark reverb.
* **Voices** (`voices.py`): built-in espeak-ng formant voices (no MBROLA, no voice cloning, no imitation of
  any real person). The whispers, murmurs and conversations use random nonsense syllables, cut up and
  partly reversed, so they cannot be understood. `voice.phrase`, `voice.announce` and `voice.radio` speak the
  short original lines given in the brief. `voice.cry` uses no TTS: it is a formant-synthesised vowel, heavily
  distanced. `skinwalker.screech` uses espeak-ng only for a sustained pure vowel (phoneme input, no words),
  pitch-dragged, ring-modulated and saturated under a synthesised shriek.
* **Creature** (`sfx.py`, `voices.py`): the skinwalker's bone cracks, joint pops, chitin clicks and leg taps are
  modal resonator banks excited by short noise impulses; nothing is recorded and nothing is gory speech.
* **Music** (`music.py`): composed procedurally from chord progressions and voicings written for this
  project, with seeded motif-based melodies whose strong beats snap to chord tones. The instruments are all
  synthesised: an additive piano with inharmonic stretched partials, two detuned strings per note and
  per-note tuning drift; an FM Rhodes; detuned band-limited saw pads; an FM celesta/bell; a sine bass; noise
  brushes. Post-processing adds chorus, ping-pong echo, synthetic hall reverb, tape wow/flutter, gentle tanh
  saturation and a hiss floor. The office radio pieces also get an AM-radio band-limit with static and crackle.
* **Determinism**: every sound is seeded from its id and variant index, and ffmpeg runs with `+bitexact`, so a
  re-run reproduces identical OGG files and identical zip SHA-1s.

### Format and level conventions
* 44.1 kHz Ogg Vorbis (libvorbis, `-q:a 3` for SFX/voices, `-q:a 2` for music).
* Mono for everything except `music.plains_N.ambient`, which is stereo and non-positional.
* Ambience loops are normalised to an integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770, gated) of about -24 LUFS. Some are
  intentionally quieter: `hum.mains` -28, `vent.small` -27, `room.damp` -32, `wind.plains` -30,
  `skinwalker.chitter` about -26 (measured on the decoded file). Music is at
  -20 LUFS with a -1.5 dBFS ceiling. One-shot dry takes peak at -3 dBFS. Profile versions are never louder than
  that, and muffled versions are about 6 dB down.
* Loops are exactly 8, 10 or 12 s (`skinwalker.chitter`: 4 s, a short creature loop), with 300 ms fades at both ends for a 6-tick overlapping re-trigger. Noise
  components use equal-power (sin/cos) fades. Tonal components are exactly periodic over `length - 0.3 s` and
  use complementary sin^2/cos^2 fades, so a 300 ms overlap of two instances rebuilds the hum seamlessly instead
  of giving a +3 dB bump. `build_pack.py` simulates that overlap for every loop.

## Research notes (general knowledge only; nothing was downloaded)

The brief names mood references. Their URLs were not reachable from the build sandbox, and by design nothing
was fetched, sampled, transcribed or quoted. Only broad, commonly described characteristics were used:

* **"School Rooftop"** (the slowed, reverb-heavy version that circulates widely in liminal-space and Backrooms
  videos): dreamy, nostalgic electronic/lo-fi with soft synth pads and bell-like lead tones, a slowed tempo,
  large washes of reverb, gentle tape-like pitch softness, and simple, consonant, mostly major harmony with
  added colour tones. Used for: `music.pool_1/2` (pads, celesta bell, long reverb, space). No melody, chord
  sequence or rhythm was taken from it.
* **"Six Forty Seven"** and **"Numbers"** (named in the brief for the plains music): treated only as
  "soft, nostalgic synth/Rhodes chords heard from a distance". Their composers and contents were not verified
  offline, and nothing about them beyond that mood descriptor was used. Used for: `music.plains_1/2` (Rhodes
  chords, pad, sparse melody, distant low-passed mix).
* The brief mentions four reference tracks. Only the three titles above appear in it, so the fourth could not
  be identified and was not used.
* **Liminal / dreamcore / vaporwave ambience in general**: slowed tempos (50 to 70 BPM here); detuned,
  tape-warped keys (wow at 0.4 to 0.6 Hz, flutter at about 6 Hz, slow pitch drift); heavy, dark reverb;
  sparse harmony built on major-7th, add9, minor-9th and maj7#11 chords and plain IV-iii-ii-V or I-vi-IV-V
  motion; lo-fi saturation and a hiss floor; and a "distant" mix (low-passed, wet, quiet, sometimes heard
  "through a wall" or "from another room").
  These are genre conventions, not protected expression. All pieces here are new compositions in common keys
  (Db, C, E, F, Bb, G, Eb, C#m) with progressions and melodies generated for this project.

## Adding your own (properly licensed) tracks

1. Make sure you have the rights. CC0 or CC-BY tracks you may redistribute, or your own work. The pack is sent
   to every client, so you are redistributing the file.
2. Convert the track to Ogg Vorbis at 44.1 or 48 kHz. Use mono if it will play positionally (radios,
   speakers) and stereo only if it plays at the listener. For example:
   `ffmpeg -i track.wav -ac 1 -ar 44100 -c:a libvorbis -q:a 2 pack/java/assets/backrooms/sounds/user/my_track.ogg`
   Keep peaks below -0.5 dBFS (`build_pack.py` rejects clipping). About -20 LUFS matches the built-in music.
3. Declare the track in `pack/user_sounds.json`. Create the file if it does not exist:
   ```json
   { "music.user_lobby": { "files": ["user/my_track.ogg"], "category": "music",
                           "positional": true, "loop": false,
                           "license": "CC-BY-4.0, Jane Doe, https://example.org/track" } }
   ```
   `generate.py` never deletes `sounds/user/`.
4. Rebuild with `python3 tools/audio/build_pack.py`. This validates the file, adds the event to `sounds.json`,
   `sound_definitions.json` and `pack-index.json`, writes a row with your license into the table below, and
   rebuilds both zips. Every zip change changes the pack SHA-1, so upload the new zip to wherever
   `resource-pack.url` points.
5. Configuration: the id appears in `pack-index.json` with `"category": "music"`. Whether, and where, the plugin
   plays extra music ids is decided by the plugin's audio engine and `audio.music*` settings in `config.yml`.
   See the plugin README for the current behaviour.
