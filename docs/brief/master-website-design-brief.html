01 / How to use this brief

Order of authority. (1) The client's own words in the current request. (2) The client's brand system and approved facts. (3) This brief. (4) The builder's own taste. When two instructions conflict, name the conflict and ask which wins; never silently pick one.

What it is for. Any site that must explain something real (a school, a product range, a service) and still feel memorable. The worked example throughout is the ISP website: 31 pages, a block-based editor, three signature motion scenes and a measured, tested motion system.

What to deliver with any build. Responsive implementation; design tokens in one file; a component map; a motion trigger table (effect, trigger, values, fallback); an editor guide; real or licensed assets with sources; explicit disclosure of anything unfinished, placeholder or unverified; and evidence of the acceptance checks in chapter 13.

Facts are never invented. Names, numbers, dates, fees, results and claims come only from client-supplied sources. Anything awaiting confirmation is labelled in the content (for example "Awaiting school verification") and in a sources document, not guessed.

02 / Ten principles

1
Understand before spectacle. A calm grid, clear words and real photographs carry the information; motion adds meaning or atmosphere, never the only path to content.
2
Few signatures, well made. At most one signature scene per page (spiral gallery, sideways chapter or lit statement), separated by calm content.
3
Motion lives in the background. Headings and body text stay still once read. Prefer slow, low-contrast ambient movement (faint labels, gentle parallax) over moving the thing people are reading.
4
Nothing jumps. Animated components reserve their space. A caption, counter or card swapping content must never move the page.
5
Everything visible, everything loaded. No content hidden behind a reveal that might not fire; no image left unloaded because an animation hid it.
6
Every control goes somewhere. No dead links or "page not found" from navigation. Features that need a server explain themselves in previews.
7
Less text, better text. Lead with what the reader needs; trim to icon + name + one line where a list is scanned; offer "Read more" for long passages.
8
Accessible by construction. Semantic HTML first, keyboard and screen-reader parity, reduced-motion versions designed rather than bolted on.
9
Editors are users too. Every visual option is a clear choice in the editor with drafts, live preview, roles and version history.
10
Prove it. Ship with automated tests, accessibility scans, overflow checks at every width and a link and image crawl of the deployed or exported site.

05 / Architecture and storytelling

Pages that explain before they impress

Adapted from the research's purpose-led storytelling model: state the human outcome, show the thing in its real environment, name it with one benefit, then give a direct route.

Home page flow (default)

  1. Promise + spatial hero: one sentence of outcome, two actions, and the spiral gallery of real photographs (about one viewport). A down-arrow cue invites scrolling.
  2. Fact strip: three or four verified facts with sources.
  3. Journey or comparison chapter: the one sideways chapter, telling a real progression (ISP: Early Years → Primary → Secondary → Sixth Form).
  4. Purposeful showcases: a feature mosaic of three to five areas, each linking to its page.
  5. Lit statement: two to four words that define the brand, lit one at a time.
  6. Evidence: latest stories, results or proof.
  7. Direct action: visit, enquire or buy, with addresses and contact.

Interior pages

Hero (page, compact or stage variant) → introduction → two to five content sections from the block library → related links → one call-to-action band. Breadcrumbs on every page below the top level. Each audience has a direct route within one click of the home page.

Content rules

  • Headings describe the content ("How to apply", not "Your journey begins").
  • Link text makes sense on its own; dates read like "Saturday 14 November 2026"; times like "9:00am – 11:00am".
  • Scanned lists: icon + term + one line. Long passages: "Read more". Facts: dated and sourced.
  • No student personal data. Alternative text describes what is happening and never names children.
06 / Motion

Three levels, one owner, calm by default

Level 1 is feedback (150–300 ms). Level 2 is reveal and ambient motion (450–900 ms, or linked to scroll position). Level 3 is the signature scenes: at most one per page, separated by calm content. One shared, frame-throttled scroll listener owns all scroll-driven effects. No smooth-scroll libraries; never capture the wheel.

Avoid list (tried and rejected)

  • Scroll-linked sideways movement of headings or titles; stack, spread and side-exit title choreography.
  • Clip-path "curtains" that fully hide images; any reveal that leaves content at opacity 0 if a script fails.
  • Cross-document view transitions and page-enter transforms on main (blank pages in embedded viewers).
  • Multi-hue gradient progress bars; visible gallery control clutter; autoplaying carousels without pause.

07 / Layout stability

Nothing jumps, nothing is lost

  • RULE Animated components reserve fixed slots for variable text (fixed height, line clamp, fixed grid rows). Test by cycling every state and asserting the next section's position does not change.
  • RULE Images declare width and height; aspect-ratio frames; focal point for crops.
  • RULE Pinned sections compute their scroll length from measured overflow, re-measure on resize, fonts and images, and never pin when there is nothing to scroll.
  • RULE No horizontal page scroll at any width: clip decorative overflow on main; only tables and code scroll inside their own containers.
  • RULE Server and browser must render the same markup (no hydration mismatch). Any component that reads the URL must also accept static-file addresses.
  • RULE The first hero never animates in (fast largest-contentful paint); its key image is preloaded.
08 / Components and the editor

Blocks people can use

Pages are ordered stacks of blocks. Every visual option above is an editor choice, not code:

HeroHome (spiral gallery and cue), Page (large photo), Learning stage (age label and colour), Compact.
Terms and descriptionsGrid with descriptions, or Spiral (icon, term, tagline); optional icon per item from a curated set.
Link cards / Numbered stepsGrid or Sideways row.
TextAside, narrow or statement; signature and portrait make it a sticky letter; long text gets Read more automatically.
OthersImage and text, Fact strip, Learning journey, Feature mosaic, Lit statement, Grouped lists, Table, Gallery, People, Documents, FAQ list, Latest stories, Upcoming events, Campuses, Admissions information, Enquiry form, Notice, Call to action, Word band, Reusable section.

Editor requirements

  • Drafts with autosave; Publish is explicit; version history with restore; scheduled expiry for banners and events.
  • Live preview beside the form at phone, tablet and desktop sizes; drafts visible only to signed-in staff.
  • Roles: Contributor (drafts only), Editor (publish, menus), Admissions (enquiries, events, fees), Administrator (everything, staff). The last administrator cannot be demoted.
  • Media library: alternative text, approval status, release reference, focal point, automatic web sizes.
  • Global settings edited once and reused (contacts, campuses, fees, menus, announcement).
  • Enquiries: created only by the site's forms; status workflow; never exposed through the public API.
  • A plain-English editor guide, plus a tour of real screens for staff without access yet.

10 / Accessibility and reduced motion

Same content, every way in

  • Honour prefers-reduced-motion in CSS and JavaScript, live: no pinning, parallax, spiral turning, drifting labels or bands; everything stays in normal flow.
  • Buttons for actions, links for navigation; 44×44px targets; visible 3px focus ring (accent colour; highlight colour on dark sections).
  • Signature scenes keep a semantic list of their items; galleries reachable by keyboard; skip links past every pinned section.
  • Decorative duplicates (rolling text, background labels, "@" shapes) hidden from assistive technology and drawn as CSS-generated content where scanners would read them as text.
  • Dialogs: native modal, inert background, Escape closes, focus restored. Live regions for captions and form status.
  • Forms: labels, inline validation on blur, an error summary, preserved answers, honeypot and minimum completion time; consent-gated analytics and maps.
  • Scrollable regions are keyboard-focusable. Zero serious or critical axe violations on representative pages.
11 / Performance, images and hosting

Fast first, then rich

  • Responsive WebP sizes (400/800/1400/2200) and a 1200×630 share image; sizes set per component; width and height on every image.
  • Preload only the first hero image. Lazy-load below the fold, except inside moving or pinned scenes, which load eagerly.
  • Animate transform and opacity only; one scroll owner; requestAnimationFrame loops stop when settled.
  • Ship only the icons used; validate forms with a small schema library shared by browser and server.
  • Cache published content and invalidate on publish; drafts bypass the cache.
  • Security headers: CSP limited to the third parties actually used (consent-gated); force https and HSTS only when the site is served over https.
  • Targets: Lighthouse accessibility, best practices and SEO 100; performance at least 85 on mobile for key pages.

Static previews and embedded viewers

When a build is exported as plain files (for review links or artifact viewers): map server routes to files; build search from an exported index; make server-only features (staff sign-in, form sending) explain themselves; open each page at the top; retry dropped images; keep structured data byte-identical to avoid hydration mismatches; and crawl every link and image before sharing.

12 / Implementation contract

Build it this way

  • Semantic, server-rendered HTML first; enhance progressively. Content stays visible without JavaScript.
  • One source of truth per scene (progress value, selected item); renderer separate from data.
  • Native vertical scrolling; sticky viewports; no wheel interception, no competing smooth-scroll libraries.
  • Tokens in one file; one component per block (schema + renderer); database migrations for every schema change.

14 / Paste-ready prompt

Give an AI this

Build a production website and a block-based editor for [CLIENT] from the attached facts, brand assets and
information architecture. Follow the attached Master Website Design Brief exactly; my words override it.

Style: calm, editorial and purposeful, with real photography and the brand's own colours and type. Make the
subject understandable before it is spectacular. Motion should feel alive but stay in the background: headings
fade up once and then stay still; ambient motion uses faint background labels and gentle photo parallax.

Signature scenes, at most one per page: (1) a hero spiral photo gallery turned by page scroll, drag and arrow
keys, with no visible gallery buttons and a fixed two-line caption; (2) one sideways chapter that pins only
when its row overflows, aligned to the content edge, otherwise a snap swipe row; (3) a lit statement where
words brighten in turn with an underline, never sliding sideways. Values and feature lists: icon + term +
one-line tagline (optional spiral). Long text: Read more with a down arrow. Sticky portrait beside letters.

Never: move headings sideways with scroll; hide images behind clip-path curtains; use page transitions; use
multi-hue progress bars; leave any link pointing to a missing page; invent facts.

Deliver: tokens, component map, motion trigger table, editor with drafts, live preview, roles and versions,
migrations, editor guide, sources document, and evidence of every check in the acceptance checklist.
Disclose anything unfinished or unverified.
15 / Sources and limits

Where this comes from

  • Website Design Research: Editorial clarity, spatial motion, purposeful products (22 October 2026): five live references (spatial explorer, kinetic titles, horizontal chapter, Inertia's interaction language, Floema's storytelling). This brief keeps its storytelling model, three motion levels, horizontal model and accessibility requirements.   • The International School @ ParkCity build (Payload CMS in Next.js, PostgreSQL), October 2026: every value in the motion catalogue, the responsive table and the checklist is implemented and covered by automated tests (74 end-to-end tests, including axe scans), overflow checks at nine widths, and link and image crawls of an exported static preview.
  • Owner review rounds, 23 October 2026: the corrections in chapter 3.
Limits. Real-device testing on iOS Safari inside the client's own viewer was reported by the owner rather than reproduced in automation; the Safari-specific fixes (eager images in moving scenes, no curtains, no view transitions) are reasoned from those reports.
